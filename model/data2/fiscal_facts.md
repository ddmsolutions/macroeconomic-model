## 1. Fiscal rules in force (2026)

**Stability rule (fiscal mandate, current budget)** [V]
- Charter wording: "the current budget must be in surplus in 2029-30, until 2029-30 becomes the third year of the forecast period. From that point, the current budget must then remain in balance or in surplus from the third year of the rolling forecast periods" (Charter s3.6).
  - Source: Charter for Budget Responsibility (updated, presented Feb 2026): https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69778bb967ae94b32801385b/260122_-_Updated_Charter_-_Jan_2026_changes.pdf
- OBR Nov 2025 EFO describes it as "the current budget to be in balance in 2029-30". Source: https://obr.uk/docs/dlm_uploads/OBR_Economic_and_fiscal_outlook_November_2025.pdf
- Current budget = receipts minus current (non-investment) spending, i.e. PSNB excluding net investment.

**Investment rule (supplementary target, PSNFL)** [V]
- Charter wording: PSNFL must be "falling as a share of the economy by 2029-30, until 2029-30 becomes the third year of the forecast period. Debt should then fall by the third year of the rolling forecast period" (s3.7). Same Charter URL.
- Institute for Government summary: "Net financial debt should fall as a share of the economy in 2029/30", rolling to the third year of the forecast later. https://www.instituteforgovernment.org.uk/explainer/current-fiscal-rules

**Target year**
- November 2025 Budget: both rules assessed in **2029-30** [V] (OBR Nov 2025 EFO, URL above). No change to the target year found in the November 2025 Budget [V: Wikipedia summary "balancing the current budget by 2029/30", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2025_United_Kingdom_budget].
- March 2026 spring forecast: headroom again measured in **2029-30** (see §2) [V].
- Autumn 2026 Budget (28 October 2026): Resolution Foundation (22 Sep 2026) still calls 2029-30 "the binding fiscal rule year" [V]. https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/healey-faces-budget-balancing-act-as-rising-inflation-begins-to-hit-home/
- **Assessment frequency changed:** the OBR now assesses the rules only once a year, at the Budget. The March 2026 EFO states: "This EFO therefore does not include an assessment of performance against the fiscal rules." The OBR still publishes a second five-year forecast each spring [V]. https://obr.uk/efo/economic-and-fiscal-outlook-march-2026/
- The Charter also commits to "one major fiscal event a year, unless in the case of an economic shock" (s3.13) [V]. The welfare cap still applies (s3.8).

## 4. Debt interest sensitivities and debt structure

**Headline sensitivities (whole-forecast, OBR)** [V]
- March 2026 EFO para 1.21: "A sustained 1 percentage point decrease in Bank Rate and gilt yields would reduce borrowing by **£15 billion in 2030-31**. A 1 percentage point increase would raise borrowing by a similar amount." https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69a6d7b62e1f4fbda4252208/economic-and-fiscal-outlook-march-2026-web-accessible.pdf
- Nov 2025 EFO para 1.27: a 1pp increase in Bank Rate and gilt yields "would reduce the current surplus by **£16 billion in 2029-30**". https://obr.uk/docs/dlm_uploads/OBR_Economic_and_fiscal_outlook_November_2025.pdf
- Other March 2026 scenarios: productivity growth of 0.5% a year → borrowing about £40bn higher in 2030-31. Productivity growth of 1.5% → about £50bn lower. Nov 2025: 0.5% productivity → current budget deficit of £6bn in 2029-30 instead of a £22bn surplus.
- An IFS slide citing OBR: if unemployment settles at 5.5%, borrowing could be £21bn higher in 2030-31. https://ifs.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/The%20Spring%20Forecast%20in%20perspective%20[slides%20for%20web%20-%20updated].pdf

- **[INF]** Gilt plus short rates in 2028-29 comes to about £14.5bn, consistent with the OBR headline of £15–16bn. The short-rate effect falls over time as QT shrinks APF reserves, while the gilt-rate effect rises as the debt stock rolls over.

**Debt structure** [V]
- Index-linked share: **25.2%** of the government's wholesale debt portfolio (end-Dec 2025, about £688.5bn uplifted nominal). Source: DMO Debt Management Report 2026-27, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/debt-management-report-2026-27/debt-management-report-2026-27. The Commons Library puts it at "around three quarters conventional".
- Average maturity of the gilt stock: **13.9 years** at end-Dec 2025 (conventionals 12.9, index-linked 16.8), per the DMO DMR 2026-27. NIESR quotes **13.4 years** at end-Dec 2025 (down from 16.5 ten years earlier): https://niesr.ac.uk/blog/gilts-are-getting-shorter-does-it-matter. The difference probably reflects weighting (uplifted vs nominal) [INF].
- 2026-27 gilt remit: £252.1bn total. Short £97.3bn (38.6%), medium £77.8bn, long £23.0bn (9.1%), index-linked £23.5bn (9.3%), unallocated £30.5bn. Gross financing requirement £273.4bn (DMR 2026-27).
- APF / Bank of England holdings:
  - The BoE holds "around 18% of gilts, down from a peak of about 34% in 2022" (Commons Library CBP-10842, 24 Jun 2026).
  - APF stock £521.8bn at 30 Jun 2026 (https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/asset-purchase-facility/2026/2026-q2).
  - APF stock £488bn on 16 Sep 2026 (BoE Sept 2026 minutes).
  - The DMR cites £373.3bn at end-Jan 2026. This is probably a different valuation basis [INF].
- QT: £70bn reduction over Oct 2025–Sep 2026. From Oct 2026 the plan is £20bn a year of active sales plus about £26bn of maturities (about £46bn a year), running down to zero by about Sep 2034 (BoE Sept 2026 minutes).
- **Effective maturity after QE** [V, older vintages]:
  - OBR March 2021 EFO Box 4.1: gilt mean maturity was "over 15 years" at end-2020, but QE reduced the effective mean maturity to 11 years, with about 10 years expected by end-2021-22. Median maturity including APF effects was 4 years (vs 11), and "less than one year by March 2022" including all government financing. Share of debt repricing over the forecast: 59%, against 44% pre-2009. https://obr.uk/box/debt-maturity-quantitative-easing-and-interest-rate-sensitivity/
  - OBR March 2024 EFO box: effective median maturity went from 1 year (2021) to 3 years by end-2023, with 7 years projected by 2028 as QT proceeds. https://obr.uk/box/the-sensitivity-and-volatility-of-debt-interest-spending/
  - A 2026 figure for effective maturity after QE: [NV].
- Debt interest outturn 2025-26: £109.7bn (DMR). The OBR FRS July 2026 says "£110 billion in 2025-26". https://obr.uk/frs/fiscal-risks-and-sustainability-july-2026/

**Receipts ready reckoners (OBR, March 2025 EFO vintage)** [V]
- Income tax: a +1% rise in average earnings adds **£3.5bn**, rising to **£4.4bn** by the end of the forecast. +1% employment adds £2.4bn rising to £2.8bn. +1% dividend income adds about £0.2bn and +1% self-employment income about £0.3bn. The OBR notes receipts "rise (and fall) proportionately more than changes in labour income". https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/tax-by-tax-spend-by-spend/income-tax/
- NICs: +1% average earnings adds about **£2.8bn** a year and +1% employment about £2.1bn (average over the forecast). https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/tax-by-tax-spend-by-spend/national-insurance-contributions-nics/
- Base: income tax £329bn and NICs £205.4bn in 2025-26 (same pages).
- **[INF]** Income tax plus NICs therefore gain about £6.3–7.2bn per 1% on average earnings. That is about 1.2% of the combined £534bn base, so the elasticity of IT+NICs to earnings is about 1.2. The PAYE-only elasticity is higher, because part of the income tax base is not earnings. The OBR publishes no explicit elasticity coefficient [NV].
- An OBR rule of thumb for total receipts versus nominal GDP: [NV].
- Long-run context: OBR FRS July 2026 says uprating thresholds with CPI rather than freezing them changes the long-run receipts path by about 6pp of GDP. https://obr.uk/frs/fiscal-risks-and-sustainability-july-2026/
